Research and policy

Our founder’s research tests whether food recovery can be financially sustainable, and which Illinois policies would help organizations like ours scale.

Download the research paper (PDF)

Citation: Jibrail, Muhammad. 2026. The Bridge Pantry: A Financially Sustainable Model for Reducing Food Waste and Homelessness in Chicago. Harris School of Public Policy, The University of Chicago.

  • $1.44

    Cost per meal, modeled.A sponsored refrigerated truck could deliver about 93,600 meals a year at roughly $1.44 per meal in cash costs ($1.33 for a leaner van pilot), below the $2.07 per meal that SNAP provides in Illinois.

  • 36%

    Of operating costs covered by sponsorship alone.Advertising-style sponsorship will not close the gap by itself, so we are building a blended model of sponsors, restaurant partnerships, grants, and individual giving.

  • 0.24%

    Of Illinois foodservice surplus donated in 2024.We propose a tiered edible-food recovery requirement for large generators, clearer food-safety guidance, and donor-liability protection aligned with federal law.

  • SNAP

    Complement, not replace.Food recovery stretches the public dollar furthest for the people SNAP reaches least. It should sit alongside public benefits, never in place of them.
