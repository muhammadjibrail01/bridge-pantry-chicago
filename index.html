What we do

Bridge Pantry Chicago connects food that would be wasted with neighbors who need a meal, quickly, safely, and with dignity. From food rescue pickups to community food support, every program starts with one idea: safe food should feed people, not landfills.

  • Food Recovery

    Rescuing safe, unsold food from local food providers.

  • Community Support

    Improving food access for students and residents.

  • Sustainability

    Reducing preventable food waste.

  • Partnerships

    Working collaboratively with restaurants and organizations.

Chicago throws away safe food every night. Neighbors go without.

Restaurants close with trays of prepared food that will be thrown out within the hour. A few blocks away, people sleeping under bridges and along the river go without a hot meal. Bridge Pantry Chicago closes that gap the same night.

Read the research behind these numbers

  • 36% of Chicago households were food insecure in June 2026. Greater Chicago Food Depository, Hunger in Chicago 2026
  • 1,641 people were unsheltered on one January night in 2026, about a quarter more than the year before. City of Chicago DFSS, 2026 Point-in-Time count
  • 0.24% of Illinois restaurant and foodservice surplus was donated in 2024. Nearly all the rest was landfilled. ReFED Insights Engine, 2026
  • 18.8% fewer people on Illinois SNAP in the twelve months to June 2026. USDA SNAP data tables, 2026

How it works

Three steps take food from a restaurant kitchen to a neighbor's hands, usually within hours.

  1. 1

    Restaurant Partnerships

    Partner restaurants identify safe surplus food that was prepared but not sold.

  2. 2

    Food Recovery

    Bridge Pantry Chicago coordinates pickups through volunteers and community partners.

    Pickup schedules may occur:
    • Daily
    • Several times per week
    • Weekly

    depending on food availability and partner needs.

  3. 3

    Community Distribution

    Recovered food is redistributed through organized community support efforts.

    Today that means evening meals for neighbors experiencing homelessness in downtown Chicago and Hyde Park, with extra food shared through community refrigerators and partner food banks.

Food Safety Commitment

Bridge Pantry Chicago does not collect food leftovers from customers or partially consumed meals.

All recovered food comes directly from approved food providers and participating restaurants and is collected according to responsible food recovery practices.

  • Partner staff pack unsold, safe food in sealed containers.
  • Volunteers collect it in short, scheduled pickup windows.
  • Food is sorted and distributed the same evening.
