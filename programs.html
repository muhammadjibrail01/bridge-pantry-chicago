Programs

Five connected programs that turn surplus food into community support across Chicago.

From kitchen to community

Food recovery and food redistribution across Chicago. We work downtown, in the Loop and adjacent neighborhoods, and in Hyde Park, with a weekly partner route that reaches north to Lakeview.

Active

Food Recovery Network

Coordinating surplus food collection.

We coordinate evening pickups of unsold, safe food from partner restaurants, other food providers, and campus events, then get it to neighbors the same night.

  • Eight Nando’s PERi-PERi locations release surplus on a weekly evening route through the chain’s Harvest Program.
  • Pickup schedules may be daily, several times per week, or weekly, depending on food availability and partner needs.
  • Food is packed in sealed containers by partner staff and collected within a short pickup window.

Harvest Program route

  • Hyde Park
  • South Loop
  • Michigan Avenue
  • Wabash
  • Streeterville
  • West Loop
  • Clybourn
  • Lakeview

What we ask of a partner location

  • About eight to ten meals, or one tray, per pickup
  • A named staff contact
  • Sealed containers and a 30 to 45 minute pickup window
  • No cost to the restaurant

Community Distribution

Connecting resources with individuals and families.

Recovered meals are delivered the same evening to neighbors experiencing homelessness, under bridges and along transit corridors downtown and in Hyde Park. Extra food is routed to community refrigerators and partner food banks.

  • Prepared, ready-to-eat meals delivered the same night.
  • Dignity first: no photos of neighbors without informed consent.
  • Planned: referrals to SNAP enrollment, ID services, and housing navigation.
In development

Student Support Initiative

Improving food access among students.

Students face rising food costs while campus events and nearby restaurants throw away safe food. We are designing student food assistance for Chicago with discreet access to recovered meals and connections to campus and city resources.

Students who want to help shape it are welcome. Tell us what would help.

Growing

Community Partnerships

Collaborating with local organizations and food providers.

We work with restaurants, cafes, caterers, grocers, universities, and community organizations. Our approach is to strengthen Chicago’s existing food-rescue system rather than duplicate it.

  • Free, flexible pickups built around your closing routine.
  • Simple written agreements and one named point of contact.
  • Start small: many partners begin with one weekly pickup.

Volunteer Engagement

Mobilizing community members for impact.

Volunteers power everything we do today: pickups, sorting, distribution, outreach, data, operations, and communications. We value volunteer time as a real contribution and build roles that are clear and flexible.

Where we’re headed

Four stages, each started only when the one before it is producing reliable results. Timing depends on funding, partners, and approvals.

  1. Stage 1 Now

    Volunteer food rescue

    Evening collection and distribution from restaurant partners and campus events, using volunteers’ own vehicles.

    Active
  2. Stage 2 2027

    Refrigerated recovery vehicle

    A refrigerated vehicle holds food at safe temperatures and extends our routes, supported by sponsors, restaurant partners, grants, and individual giving.

    Planned
  3. Stage 3 2028

    Policy and advocacy

    Research-backed proposals for Illinois: edible-food recovery requirements for large generators, clearer food-safety guidance, and aligned donor-liability protections.

    Planned
  4. Stage 4 2029 to 2030

    Reintegration and social enterprise

    Services, job training, and housing navigation built on the trust of the nightly route, in partnership with existing providers.

    Planned
